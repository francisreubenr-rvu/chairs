What is Entrepreneurship E&I p.2

Forming a new enterprise where growth is possible

  • The practice of forming a new business or commercial enterpriseUsually in an industry or sector of the economy with a large capacity for growth
  • ResourcefulnessEntrepreneurship is generally synonymous with resourcefulness
  • IngenuityAnd with ingenuity
  • Calculated riskThe ability to take calculated risks in order to introduce a new, untested product or service into the marketplace
Note the word "calculated". The definition does not say risk-taking. It says calculated risk: the risk is priced before it is taken. That distinction is what an examiner and a hostile delegate will both probe.
"Large capacity for growth" is part of the definition The source does not define entrepreneurship as starting any business. It specifies a sector with a large capacity for growth, and a product or service that is new and untested. Both conditions matter when you argue that your project qualifies.
What is Innovation E&I p.3

Renewing a domain, product or service to create new value

  • The process by which a domain, a product or a service is renewed and brought up to date
  • The meansBy applying new processes, introducing new techniques, or establishing successful ideas, to create new value
Innovation ends in "new value", not in "new thing". Novelty that creates no value is not innovation under this definition. That is the standard your project will be judged against.
Trainer practice Ask each team to finish the sentence "this creates value for ___ because ___" before they present. Teams that cannot fill both blanks have a novelty, not an innovation.
Three routes the definition names
New processesDoing the same thing a different way
New techniquesNew capability applied to the work
Established ideasSomething already proven, brought somewhere it was not
The third route is the underrated one "Establishing successful ideas" means an approach that already works elsewhere counts as innovation when brought into a domain that lacks it. Your project does not have to be unprecedented. It has to create new value here.
Innovation is crucial to us E&I p.3

It starts as a shift in mind-set

The source is specific about the direction of travel: innovation flows from shifts in mind-set. The mind-set comes first, the output second.

  • Shifts in mind-set generate new business models
  • They recognise new opportunities
  • They weave innovations throughout the fabric of society
  • It is about new ways of doing and seeing things as much as it is about the breakthrough idea
"As much as it is about the breakthrough idea." This is the line that lets a modest, well-executed project beat a spectacular one. You do not need a breakthrough. You need a genuinely different way of doing or seeing.
Three things a mind-set shift generates, per the source
New business modelsHow the thing sustains itself
New opportunitiesRecognised, not invented
Innovations woven through societyNot confined to a product
Types of innovation Added · named frameworks

Four kinds, four different arguments

Knowing which kind you are proposing tells you who will resist it and why.

TypeWhat changesWho resists it
IncrementalAn existing product or process gets measurably betterFew. Easiest to fund, hardest to differentiate.
SustainingImproves performance for existing, usually demanding, customersNobody, which is why incumbents are good at it
DisruptiveStarts cheaper or simpler, serves an overlooked segment, then moves upIncumbents, who rationally ignore it until it is too late
Radical / breakthroughNew technology creating a market that did not existRegulators and existing standards bodies
Sustaining and disruptive come from Clayton Christensen's work on why well-run incumbent firms lose to entrants. If your solution is disruptive, expect the industry association in your committee to oppose it. That is the model's prediction, and you can say so out loud.
Why this matters in a committee of companies If your solution is sustaining, incumbents will support it, because it makes their existing offer better. If it is disruptive, expect the industry association to oppose it, because associations speak for incumbents. Naming which kind you are proposing lets you predict the room before you enter it.
The project focus E&I p.4 Added

Creativity, Feasibility, Impact

The source names three criteria for the project. They correspond closely to the desirability–feasibility–viability triad used in design and venture practice.

Creativity
  • Thinking ideas out of the box
  • Test: could another team have produced this in five minutes without research? If yes, it is not yet creative.
  • Related lens: desirability: does anyone actually want it?
Feasibility
  • Could this idea work in real life?
  • Test: name the materials, the people and the money. Vagueness here is the commonest failure.
  • Related lens: viability: can it sustain itself past launch?
Impact
  • How will it help people or the environment?
  • Test: impact on whom, at what scale, measured how?
  • Ties directly to the SDG you were assigned
The project focus · missing a lens E&I p.4 Added

Which pair you have tells you what to fix

An idea has to clear all three criteria. The two you already hold identify the question you have not yet answered.

What each pair alone produces
Creative + feasible, no impactA clever thing nobody needed. Busywork.
Creative + impactful, not feasibleA wish. It presents well and cannot be built.
Feasible + impactful, not creativeProbably already tried. Find out by whom, and why it stalled.
An idea has to clear all three. The pair you are missing tells you which question to go and answer next.
From idea to venture Added · named frameworks

Six questions between an idea and a working venture

These map onto the presentation brief on the next slides. Answer them in this order.

  1. Problem: what specifically is broken, for whomStated as a problem, not as the absence of your solution
  2. Customer / beneficiary: who is most affected, and how it shapes their daySegment it. "Everyone" is not a segment.
  3. Value proposition: what you offer, and why it beats the current alternativeThe current alternative is usually "do nothing", which is free and therefore hard to beat
  4. Minimum viable version: the smallest build that tests the riskiest assumptionFrom Eric Ries's lean startup method: build, measure, learn
  5. Resources: materials, tools, skills, money, partnersThis is where feasibility becomes checkable
  6. Growth path: how it reaches more people if it worksDistribution is a design decision, not an afterthought
New business models E&I p.3 Added · Osterwalder

What a business model has to specify

The source says shifts in mind-set "generate new business models". A business model is not the idea; it is how the idea keeps existing after launch. The Business Model Canvas, developed by Alexander Osterwalder, names the parts.

Who you serve
  • Customer segments
  • Customer relationships
  • Channels
What you offer
  • Value propositions
  • Key activities
  • Key resources
  • Key partners
How it sustains
  • Revenue streams
  • Cost structure
For an SDG project, "revenue" may be grant funding, public budget or cross-subsidy. It still has to be named. A solution with no funding mechanism is a proposal for someone else to pay, and the committee will ask who.

Contents for presentation E&I p.6

Eight questions your project must answer

  1. What is the problem your team is trying to solve related to your SDG?
  2. Who is most affected by this problem, and how does it impact their daily life?
  3. What is your team's idea for a product or service that could help solve this problem?
  4. What makes your idea unique or different from what already exists?
  5. What materials or tools will you need to build or launch your idea?
  6. How will your idea help people or the planet in a simple and useful way?
  7. How will you let others know about your idea or service?
  8. If your idea is successful, how will it grow and help more people?
They map to the three criteria Questions 1–2 establish the problem and who it lands on. Questions 3–4 are your Creativity score. Questions 5 and 7 are Feasibility. Questions 6 and 8 are Impact. An answer you cannot give is a criterion you will lose marks on.
Answering the brief well Added

The weak answer and the strong answer

Every one of the eight questions has a version that sounds complete and says nothing. Here is the difference on the four that decide the result.

QuestionWeakStrong
Q2 · Who is affected"People in developing countries"A named segment, with what their day looks like now
Q4 · What makes it unique"Nothing like it exists"Names two things that do exist, and the specific gap between them
Q5 · Materials and tools"Technology and funding"An itemised list someone could go and buy
Q8 · How it grows"We will scale it up"The next segment, the next region, and what breaks at that size
Trainer practice Q4 is the one to press hardest. A team that claims nothing comparable exists has almost always not looked. Two minutes of searching usually finds a comparable, and finding it yourself is far better than an examiner finding it.
