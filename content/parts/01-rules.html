Precursor for the Conference

Three definitions every later rule assumes. Say them out loud with the examples.

  1. Portfolio: Country / Character / Company For example: Australia / Harry Potter / Google.
  2. Committee: Organisation that you represent your Portfolio in For example: WHO / Harry Potter / Internet Association.
  3. Agenda: Topic that is discussed across the 2 days of the Conference Verified wording: two days of the Conference (PDF p.2).

Virtual Delegate Kit

Pre-conference materials listed on PDF p.2. Day 3 research assumes delegates have opened these.

What the kit is not The Study Guide and Committee Brief tell you what the committee is about. They do not contain your portfolio's position. That is yours to research. A delegate who speaks only from the kit sounds identical to every other delegate who did the same.

Personal pronouns, Motions, Points

  • Use of Personal Pronouns: Address yourself and everyone as “Delegate of ___” PDF: “Address yourself and everyone as ‘Delegate of ’.”
  • Motions: the request that we raise to discuss / decide something PDF definition (p.4). Full motion list appears on Day 3 (p.11).
  • Points: used to recognise yourself in the committee PDF definition (p.4). Four types on Day 2 (p.8).
Motion or Point? The one-line test
MotionAsks the committee to do something, and is voted on
PointAsks the chair to recognise something, and is not voted on
BothAre raised by a delegate, never by the Executive Board on your behalf
The two rules that switch off, and only there Personal pronouns and electronic equipment both become permissible during an Unmoderated Caucus, and nowhere else. In every other part of the conference: third person, English, no devices.
Day 1 · Key things to remember PDF p.4

Representation

  • Language: Official language for all the communication is English
  • Electronic equipment is prohibited during the Formal Debate Scope is Formal Debate (p.4). Two rules become null and void in Unmoderated Caucus (p.9).
  • Attire for the Conference is either Western Formals / School Uniform
Trainer check · 1 min Cold call: “Is a Point the same as a Motion?” Correct answer from p.4: Motions request discuss/decide; Points recognise yourself in committee.
Three representation rules, one purpose
LanguageEnglish is the official language for all communication
DevicesElectronic equipment is prohibited during Formal Debate
AttireWestern Formals or School Uniform
Why the device rule exists In Formal Debate you are meant to be arguing from research you already did, not searching mid-speech. That is also why Point of Information requires you to name your source from memory, and why devices come back during Unmoderated Caucus, which is explicitly for research, lobbying, forming blocks and drafting.
Day 1 · Rules regarding Debate PDF p.5

Quorum

  • Quorum: Minimum delegates required = 1/5th of the Total Committee Size Verified formula only (PDF p.5). Do live arithmetic with the cohort’s actual size.
Trainer practice · 2 min Pick a size (example only): if total committee = 40, quorum = 40 × 1/5 = 8. Ask two more sizes from the room. The formula is the rule; the numbers are practice.
Presence is not agreement: three different numbers
Quorum · 1/5thOf the Total Committee Size. About who is present.
Simple Majority · 50% + 1Sub-topics, Unmod, Working Papers, Normal Amendments. About who agrees.
Super Majority · 2/3rd or 67%Resolutions only.
Why a quorum rule exists at all A decision taken by a near-empty room binds everyone who was absent. Every deliberative body from the UN General Assembly downwards therefore sets a floor before business can begin. 1/5th is this conference's floor.
Day 1 · Rules regarding Debate PDF p.5

Setting an Agenda & Speakers List

  1. Setting an Agenda - 1st Motion to be raised in the Conference This Motion is a formality to ensure the official announcement and declaration of the Agenda at the Conference, as the Agenda is given to all the delegates prior to the conference.
  2. Speakers List - 1st Speech of the Conference; mandatory for all the Delegates Functions in alphabetical order (A–Z or Z–A). Gets 90 seconds to speak.
The opening sequence, in order 1. Quorum is established at 1/5th of the Total Committee Size. 2. Motion to set the Agenda: the first Motion of the conference, and a formality, because the Agenda was given to delegates prior to the conference. 3. The Speakers List opens: alphabetical, A–Z or Z–A, 90 seconds each, mandatory for every delegate. Nothing else can happen before these three.
Day 1 · Speakers List speech PDF p.5

Two aspects that must be spoken

PDF p.5: two important aspects to be spoken in the Speakers List speech.

  1. Portfolio’s Point of View (POV) on the “Agenda”
  2. Solution on the “Agenda”
Trainer practice · 3 min One volunteer: 45 seconds only (half of 90). Must include both (1) POV and (2) Solution. Cohort marks whether both appeared.
The same two aspects appear twice On the Speakers List: POV and Solution on the Agenda. In a Moderated Caucus: POV and Solution on the Sub-Topic. Same structure, narrower scope. Learn one shape and reuse it for the whole conference.
Day 1 · Yields PDF p.6

Yields (1–2 of 4)

Used at the end of the Speaker’s List if the time is remaining. Four ways to Yield (PDF p.6).

  1. Yield to Another Delegate Only applicable if the Delegate has 15 seconds or more. Remaining time can be passed to any delegate in the same committee.
  2. Yield to Questions 2 Delegates get an opportunity to ask questions with 2 Follow ups. It is mandatory to answer the question (either verbally or via Chit).
Read "mandatory" carefully Under Yield to Questions, answering is mandatory. You choose the channel: verbally or via chit, but not whether to respond. Contrast Yield to Comments, where replying is explicitly not mandatory. This asymmetry is the most commonly missed line on page 6.
Day 1 · Yields + recap PDF p.6

Yields (3–4) & Day 1 close

  1. Yield to Comments 2 Delegates get an opportunity to comment for 30 seconds on the speech given by the Delegate. It is not mandatory to reply on the comment.
  2. Yield to the Chair Used when the speaker’s time is elapsed. Chairperson may utilize the time either by taking Questions or by Comments.
Day 1 recap (verified only): Portfolio / Committee / Agenda / Kit (p.2) · “Delegate of ___”, Motions, Points, English, no electronics in Formal Debate, attire (p.4) · Quorum 1/5, Agenda formality, Speakers List 90s with POV + Solution (p.5) · four Yields (p.6).

Day 2 · Moderated Caucus PDF p.7

Moderated Caucus: rules

Limited amount of time to discuss the Sub-Topic (PDF p.7).

  • Maximum time 20 mins
  • Can be started by any delegate in the committee
  • It is Voluntary to speak in Moderated Caucus
  • Flow for the Speaker in Caucus is decided by the Chairperson
  • Sub-Topic is decided by Simple Majority (50% + 1)
  • Delegates cannot yield the time
  • Delegate whose Motion is accepted / passed can either speak first or last

Raising the Motion & speech content

  • 3 important things to mention while raising a Motion: Sub Topic · Total Time · Time / Speaker
  • 2 important aspects to be spoken in the speech: (1) Portfolio’s Point of View (POV) on the “Sub-Topic” · (2) Solution on the “Sub-Topic”

Day 2 · Points PDF p.8

Points (1–2 of 4)

Used to recognize the Delegate. Four types of Points (PDF p.8).

  1. Point of Personal Privilege Only point that can interrupt the speaker. Used when one faces any kind of discomfort and wishes to be excused.
  2. Point of Parliamentary Inquiry Used when a delegate wants to know anything about the procedure, or have any queries regarding the debate from the Executive Board.
The interruption test Of all four Points, only Point of Personal Privilege can interrupt a delegate who is speaking. Parliamentary Inquiry, Order and Information all wait for the speaker to finish. Raising one of those mid-speech is itself a procedural error, and invites a Point of Order against you.
Day 2 · Points PDF p.8

Points (3–4 of 4)

  1. Point of Order Used to point out the Procedural Inconsistency or deviation from the existing rules of procedure.
  2. Point of Information Used to point Factual Inaccuracy. Have to prove the information by stating the source of information. Wikipedia, Quora & Chat GPT prohibited.
Trainer check · 2 min Four scenarios; students name the Point. Only Personal Privilege may interrupt (p.8). Information requires a cited source; three sources banned by name.

Day 3 · Documents PDF p.10

Working Paper & Resolution

Working Paper
  • Solution to the Subtopic
  • Has no format
  • Passed by Simple Majority (50% + 1)
  • Requires no Signatories
  • If passed, should be incorporated in Resolution
Resolution
  • Solution to the Main Agenda
  • Has a Particular Format
  • Passed by Super Majority: 2/3rd or 67%
  • Requires Signatories: 1 Author; 3 Signatories (Supporting Delegates)
  • If more than 1 DRs, one with more signatories shall be discussed first
  • To be voted via Formal Voting
Day 3 · Amendments PDF p.11

Amendments

Changes in the documents. Two types of Amendments (PDF p.11).

  1. Friendly Amendment Any kind of Punctuation Error or Spelling Mistake which doesn’t require a Majority.
  2. Normal Amendment Adding, Removing or Changing a clause from the Document which requires Simple Majority of 50% + 1.
Sort these before the session: Friendly or Normal?
FriendlyA misspelled country name
FriendlyA missing comma in a list of clauses
NormalChanging "urges" to "requires"
NormalDeleting an operative clause
NormalAdding a new obligation
The test in one question Does the change alter what the clause obliges anyone to do? If no, it is Friendly and needs no majority. If yes, it is Normal and needs Simple Majority, 50% + 1.

Day 3 · Formal Debate formats PDF p.12

Formats that don’t require majority

Types of Formal Debate which don’t require majority (PDF p.12).

  1. Challenge A delegate can challenge another delegate over a particular issue for a specified period of time; this may be over a particular sub-topic or country policy. This is one on one debate.
  2. Question Answer Session Many delegates need to ask questions to a delegate. Any delegate may call for the same and committee can ask a maximum of up to 10 questions to that delegate.
  3. One for One Against When the committee usually cannot decide on a particular issue and before the final vote needs to hear both sides of the argument.
Pick the right instrument Challenge when one delegate's position is the problem: it is one on one. Question Answer Session when many delegates need answers from one, up to 10 questions maximum. One for One Against when the committee itself is deadlocked and both sides must be heard before the final vote. None of the three requires a majority.

What is in order when the floor is crowded

If a point or motion is on the floor, anything lower on this list is out of order. The Chair rules on it, and that ruling is not subject to appeal.

1 · Procedural Points
  1. Points that may interrupt a speaker Point of Personal Privilege, the only point that can interrupt
  2. Points in order only when the floor is open Point of Information · Point of Order · Point of Parliamentary Inquiry
2 · Procedural Motions
  1. Adjournment of the MeetingHighest of the motions
  2. Suspension of the Meeting
  3. Unmoderated Caucus
  4. Moderated Caucus
  5. Introduction of a draft resolution / working paper / amendment
  6. Formal Voting
  7. Question & Answer Session
  8. ChallengeLowest of the motions
The practical rule. One thing is on the floor at a time. A delegate who raises a Moderated Caucus while an Unmoderated Caucus motion is pending is out of order, however good the reason.
Day 3 · Working paper anatomy Conference Prep p.4 sample p.7

What a working paper actually looks like

A working paper is the first attempt to put debate into written form. It is relatively formal but not bound by the resolution format, which is why it can be combined and rewritten.

Rules that govern it p.4
  • No set formatUsually a one-page proposal that focuses discussion on one aspect of the topic
  • Header carries committee name and topicSo it can be photocopied and distributed
  • Chair approval, no delegate signaturesThe paper must be approved by the Chair before it can be copied and handed out
  • Introduction can be voted informallyA show of placards is enough to introduce it
  • If passed, it must be incorporated in the resolutionThe working paper stage exists to build consensus before the resolution
Motion: "The Delegate of (country) would like to raise a motion to Introduce a Working Paper."
The sample, abridged p.7
  • Committee: UN Commission on Trade and DevelopmentTopic: Generalized System of Preferences
  • Opening: "The authors of this working paper believe that a GSP should be set up so that Least-Developed Countries receive preferential treatment from Developed Countries. To that end we propose:"
  • 1. Each DC reduces tariffs to the lowest level possible, set by a subcommittee
  • 2. Bilateral trade agreements pursue further reductions
  • 3. Preferences cover agriculture, manufactures, semi-manufactures and raw materials
  • 4. Product coverage is decided with the affected LDC, reviewed annually
  • 5. A UNCTAD subcommittee with equal developed and developing membership mediates, recommends and reports
  • 6. Five permanent DC nations, five permanent LDC nations, ten elected annually
Day 3 · Resolution rules Conference Prep p.5 sample p.8

One author, three signatories, formal vote

The resolution is the solution to the entire agenda. The numbers below hold for every council, whatever its size.

Who is who
  • AuthorFormulates the resolution and cannot vote against it
  • SignatoryConsents to the resolution being discussed, and votes as he or she pleases
  • Minimum1 author and 3 signatories
  • Competing resolutionsThe one with more signatories is put to the committee first
  • Chair approvalRequired before the resolution is introduced
  • PassingIntroduction may be voted informally; passing must use Formal Voting
How the sample is built p.8
  • HeaderCommittee, topic, full list of signatories
  • Preambulatory clausesReasons and precedent: "Recalling its Resolution A/36/89…", "Recognizing that…", "Realizing the need…"
  • Operative clausesNumbered actions: "1. Recommends…", "2) Proposes…", "3) Urges…"
  • Nested detailSub-clauses a, b, c under each operative clause
Motion: "The Delegate of (country) would like to raise a motion to Introduce a Resolution."
Day 3 · Resolution language Conference Prep p.9

Preambulatory clauses state reasons. Operative clauses state action.

Preambulatory clauses open the resolution and end in a comma. Operative clauses are numbered and end in a semicolon. A clause that starts with the wrong vocabulary reads as out of order.

Preambulatory
  • Affirming · Alarmed by · Approving · Aware of
  • Believing · Bearing in mind · Cognizant of · Confident
  • Contemplating · Convinced · Declaring
  • Deeply concerned · Deeply conscious · Deeply convinced
  • Deeply disturbed · Deeply regretting · Deploring · Desiring
  • Emphasizing · Expecting · Expressing its appreciation
  • Expressing its satisfaction · Fulfilling · Fully aware
  • Fully believing · Further deploring · Further recalling
  • Guided by · Having adopted · Having considered
  • Having considered further · Having devoted attention
  • Having examined · Having heard · Having received · Having studied
Operative
  • Accepts · Affirms · Approves · Authorizes
  • Calls for · Calls upon · Condemns · Congratulates
  • Confirms · Considers · Decides · Declares accordingly
  • Demands · Deplores · Draws attention · Designates
  • Emphasizes · Encourages · Endorses
  • Expresses its appreciation · Expresses its hope
  • Further invites · Further proclaims · Further remind
  • Further recommends · Further requests · Further resolves
  • Has resolved · Notes · Proclaims · Reaffirms
  • Recommends · Regrets · Requests · Resolves
  • Solemnly affirms · Strongly condemns · Supports
  • Takes note of · Trusts · Urges
One check before you submit Read the first word of every clause. If a numbered operative clause opens with Recognizing, or a preambulatory clause opens with Recommends, the clause is in the wrong half of the resolution.
